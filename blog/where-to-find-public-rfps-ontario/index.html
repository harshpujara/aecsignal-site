Where to find public RFPs in Ontario

Ontario’s public opportunities are spread across provincial, federal and private portals, and many cities run their own. Here’s how it fits together.

October 22, 20263 min read

There’s no single place to find public RFPs in Ontario. Opportunities are spread across a provincial portal, a federal one and several private platforms, and many cities run their own. Here’s how it fits together, so you can work out which sites your team actually needs to watch.

Provincial ministries and agencies

Ontario ministries and provincial agencies post their competitions on the Ontario Tenders Portal, which runs on a platform called Jaggaer. It’s free to view and to bid. If you’ve seen older links to a site called BravoSolution, they now lead to the same portal.

Don’t confuse it with the Ontario Vendor Portal. That one is for invoicing once you already have a contract, not for finding work.

Federal work in Ontario

Federal departments post their opportunities on CanadaBuys. If your firm does work for federal buildings, bases or infrastructure in Ontario, it belongs on your list too.

Hospitals, universities, colleges and school boards

This is where things scatter. The broader public sector, often called the MASH sector (municipalities, academic institutions, schools and hospitals), doesn’t share one portal. Each buyer picks its own, and most use one or more of these:

  • MERX, one of the longest-running tender sites in Canada
  • Biddingo, which is focused on Ontario’s broader public sector and municipal buyers
  • bids&tenders, which powers many individual organizations’ bid sites

Browsing is usually free, but some platforms charge to download documents or receive alerts. Elections Ontario, for example, tells suppliers that a subscription or a per-bid fee is needed to download its documents from MERX or Biddingo.

Cities and regions

Municipalities each choose their own system, and neighbouring cities often pick different ones. Some run their own branded bid sites (York Region, Waterloo Region and St. Catharines all have one), some post on MERX or Biddingo, and some use Euna Procurement, the platform that used to be called Bonfire.

The quickest way to find a city’s portal is to go to its website and look for a page called bids, tenders, procurement or purchasing.

Build your own list in an afternoon

  1. Write down the 20 to 30 owners you most want to work for.
  2. For each one, find where they post their bids, and note whether you need to register or pay.
  3. Register on each portal and set up alerts for the categories you work in.
  4. Give one person the job of checking the list each week, and keep it up to date as owners switch platforms.

Most teams find their list has far more sites on it than they expected, which is why so many end up paying for an aggregator that pulls them into one feed.

The catch with any portal

Every site on this page tells you about an opportunity on the day it’s posted. So does every firm’s alert. By then the owner has usually spent months planning the project, and often already has a sense of who they’d like to work with.

Portals are worth watching, but they’re the last place a project shows up. The earlier signs are in capital budgets, council agendas and permit filings. Our guide on how to read a capital budget is a good place to start.
